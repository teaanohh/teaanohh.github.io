Level design · Case study

Steampunk City & 4KOMA

Four levels designed in Mega Man Maker. Each is documented screen by screen with its purpose, mechanic cadence and a 1–5 intensity rating, then checked against a pacing curve.

Role
Solo level designer
Tool
Mega Man Maker
Scope
3 linked levels (25 screens) + a 4-screen 4KOMA
When
May – Jul 2026 · CSD2541 Level Design

The constraint

Mega Man Maker has a fixed kit of tiles, enemies and gadgets. I couldn't create new mechanics, only choose, combine and order existing ones. So the design work was in sequencing: what a player learns on each screen, when two ideas get combined, and where the tension rises and drops.

For every screen I wrote down three things:

  • Purpose: what the screen teaches or tests.
  • Cadence: which mechanics are in play, e.g. A (traversal), B (environmental interaction), C (hazards), D (enemies), A′ (timed movement).
  • Intensity: a 1–5 rating, which I plotted as a pacing curve to check the level's rhythm.

Steampunk City: a 3-level progression

The three levels follow the player down through the city, from the bright Upper District to the industrial core. Each one is harder than the last, and they're connected by a reward. The Level 1 boss drops a fire weapon, and the bosses in Levels 2 and 3 are weak to fire. Beating one level gives you the tool for the next.

Level 1: The Streets of the Upper District · Easy · 8 screens

Pacing curve

An onboarding level. Screens 1–2 are traversal only. Enemies arrive on screen 3, screen 5 dips to let the player breathe, and the level builds to the boss.

Level 2: The Ventilation Grids · Medium · 9 screens

Pacing curve

Adds timed spike-and-ladder phases, moving rail platforms and a red/blue key hunt. Peaks alternate with recovery screens before the boss.

Level 3: The Industrial Core · Hard · 8 screens

Pacing curve

Introduces conveyor belts, button-driven rails, hidden blocks, magnetic walls, springs and fan currents, one at a time, then combines them in the Core Guardian boss fight.

4KOMA: teaching 3 mechanics in 4 screens

4KOMA follows the four-panel comic structure: introduce, develop, twist, conclude. With only 4 screens, every screen had to do one job. Each of the first three introduces one new idea on its own, and the last asks the player to use them together.

Pacing curve

A: navigation · D: dodging fire traps · T: timing disappearing blocks · R: the Rush ability. The jump from 3 to 5 is deliberate, because the final screen combines everything.

What this taught me

Writing a purpose for every screen made weak screens obvious. If I couldn't say what a screen taught, it didn't belong. Plotting intensity also showed me that a good curve isn't a straight line up. Players need low points to recover before the next peak.
